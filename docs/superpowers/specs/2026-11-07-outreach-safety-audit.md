# Outreach safety audit: WhatsApp today

**Snapshot:** static source review of Colony at `7d07632852125f6518a693ea7c541a92d320744b`, 7 November 2026. This is implementation evidence, not legal advice. No Colony process, provider, production service, browser, or owner data directory was opened or run.

## One-minute summary

Colony has a useful safety core today. It stores an exact English opt-out per WhatsApp number, refuses later sends to that number, makes managed-agent messages owner-approved drafts, prevents a second outbound message before a reply, and uses a durable lease so an unknown send result is not automatically repeated. A RED Meta quality reading and selected account failures also stop relay sending.

The system is not yet safe enough for broader outreach automation. It has no cross-channel never-contact register, no business-wide reply stop, no multilingual or longer-reply opt-out matching, no owner undo, and no policy hold layer for risky links or post-undo sends. The visible Pause all controls do not reach a relay handler. Most seriously, raw WhatsApp text is mirrored into a Colony event and the generic ACP prompt formatter can render event and conversation content without the existing semantic-delimiter escaping. The owner approval gate still prevents an agent from directly sending WhatsApp, but prompt injection should be closed before Scout is given more autonomy.

The first build should restore the real pause switch and close the external-content prompt boundary. Next, build one auditable, channel-neutral suppression and business-identity foundation. It should serve WhatsApp now and email later rather than layering a separate do-not-contact mechanism on each sender.

### Policy and legal context

WhatsApp's published policy requires a business to obtain permission before contacting a person and to honor requests to block, discontinue, or opt out, including requests made outside WhatsApp. It also says user negative feedback can lead to messaging limits. WhatsApp's help material says a block or report can lead to policy review and enforcement. See the [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) and [WhatsApp help on detecting violations](https://faq.whatsapp.com/1805617343145907).

For South Africa, POPIA section 69 governs direct marketing by means of unsolicited electronic communications and sets consent or existing-customer conditions. It also requires a way for the recipient to stop such communications. See [POPIA, Act 4 of 2013, section 69](https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/2013-004.pdf) and the [Information Regulator direct-marketing guidance](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GUIDANCE-NOTE-ON-DIRECT-MARKETING-IN-TERMS-OF-THE-PROTECTION-OF-PERSONAL-INFORMATION-ACT-4-OF-2013-POPIA.pdf). Product implementation should receive South African legal review before any policy claim is represented as compliance.

## Evidence behind the controls that do exist

All tests named below were found in source and were **not run** for this audit. They are evidence of intended regression coverage, not evidence that a local, packaged, or production runtime is healthy.

### 1. Current per-number opt-out

`is_stop_message` normalizes and matches a fixed English phrase list in `policy.rs:83-105`. `inbound.rs:571-580` classifies it without an AI call, and `whatsapp_opt_out` writes `opted_out_at` and `opt_out_reason` only when no prior opt-out exists in `store/whatsapp.rs:1489-1512`. `sender::check_send` refuses that contact in `sender.rs:69-90`, and the worker repeats the check before Graph in `sender.rs:511-531`.

Pre-existing tests are `no_thanks_and_stop_are_different_opt_outs` at `policy.rs:821-836`, `stop_words_match_whole_messages_only` at `policy.rs:923-947`, and the disposable integration test `a_stop_word_opts_the_contact_out_and_later_sends_are_refused` at `postgres_tests.rs:537-577`. Those tests prove the narrow English behavior and explicitly prove that `no thanks, maybe later` does not opt out. There is no test for the requested African languages, longer-reply intent, owner undo, or complaint escalation.

No language beyond English appears in the current matcher. The Meta policy sets the obligation to honor an opt-out, but does not supply a language dictionary, so the next implementation needs product-owned, native-speaker-reviewed rules rather than an inferred translation list.

The inbound path still mirrors the raw reply, enqueues a generic inbound-reply wake, and posts a channel notice when a newly detected opt-out occurs in `inbound.rs:610-645`. That is not a dedicated owner escalation. `whatsapp_list_inbox` filters `opted_out_at IS NULL` in `store/whatsapp.rs:1554-1565`, so the ordinary Needs reply source does not include it.

### 2. Current list protection, not a never-contact registry

`plan_list_rows` skips a known opted-out WhatsApp contact with a reason in `crates/buzz-cli/src/commands/whatsapp.rs:780-843`. The test `a_list_yields_rows_for_members_with_a_number_and_explains_every_skip` at `whatsapp.rs:1295-1342` covers that one status. The relay independently refuses opted-out rows on initial send and later worker processing, including retry SQL predicates in `crates/buzz-db/src/store/whatsapp.rs:2239-2441`.

Discovery accepts phone, website, and email data at `crates/buzz-cli/src/commands/discovery.rs:175-190`, but its save-time `ExistingLeads` check only de-duplicates prospect IDs, provider IDs, and display names at `493-548`. No suppression lookup is present in that path. This audit found no channel-neutral do-not-contact model in the scoped paths.

### 3. One-contact reply fence

The existing `outbound_since_inbound` check stops a second message to the same contact in `sender.rs:86-90`. The unit test `never_two_messages_in_a_row_without_a_reply` at `sender.rs:988-1001` proves that single-contact behavior. `WhatsappContact` contains one optional `lead_id` at `store/whatsapp.rs:252-274`, and both `whatsapp_set_lead` and `whatsapp_opt_out` update by one `(community_id, wa_id)` at `1471-1512`. No sibling-contact or party-wide fan-out is present in that code path.

### 4. Existing automatic pauses and the broken manual switch

`plan_quality` decides that RED quality pauses sending and reports the owner once at `connect.rs:516-571`; `apply_phone_info` persists that decision at `574-640`. The unit tests `red_quality_stops_sending_and_tells_the_owner_once` and `better_quality_resumes_only_a_pause_quality_caused` at `connect.rs:759-795` cover that pure decision. The worker returns before listing messages when `send_paused` is true, and its claim SQL also checks the setting atomically, at `sender.rs:496-498` and `store/whatsapp.rs:2082-2109`.

The desktop's `sendingPaused` JSON field has no matching member in `SettingsCommand`, and `handle_settings` only accepts cap, template sync, title, or resume-and-retry. This is a real UI-to-relay defect, not an untested feature. The existing tests cover automatic quality decisions, not the actual pause control.

### 5. Durable at-most-once send path

The sender documents the intended contract at `sender.rs:1-9`, leases a row before the Graph call at `586-604`, and stops the pass on an unavailable outcome at `686-689`. A lease that expires becomes `unconfirmed` rather than sendable again in `store/whatsapp.rs:2591-2618`; `tick` posts an owner notice in `sender.rs:374-398`.

The disposable integration test `a_crash_between_the_graph_call_and_the_record_never_sends_twice` at `postgres_tests.rs:1927-1979` deliberately makes Graph accept a message while the relay loses its record lease. It asserts a send count of one before and after lease expiry. The test is strong relay-path evidence, but it was not run here and does not exercise desktop restart or a real provider.

`resumeAndRetry` invokes account-health recovery only for eligible failed rows in `whatsapp_commands.rs:1328-1343`; it does not make an `unconfirmed` row safe to resend. There is no audited owner recovery action that turns the stored unknown message into a fresh draft.

### 6. Prompt boundary and existing send fence

Inbound `message.body` is copied into an Outreach-channel event in `channel.rs:408-431`. `format_event_block` inserts `be.event.content` into text at `queue.rs:1368-1380`, and `format_conversation_context` inserts `msg.content` into a semantic section at `1915-1939`. Both bypass the existing `escape_semantic_text` helper, whose explicit purpose is to stop a closing delimiter from introducing a model-visible section at `prompt_framing.rs:78-89`. The helper's own test proves the helper, not the event or conversation path, at `prompt_framing.rs:116-120`.

There are meaningful narrower safeguards. The cofounder wake intentionally carries only IDs and never outsider text at `cofounder_wake.rs:24-27`, and a managed agent is never granted owner/admin send access in `whatsapp/mod.rs:26-104`. `initial_state` writes an agent message as `draft`, while only an owner/admin message starts `queued`, at `sender.rs:307-315`. The test `only_an_owner_or_admin_message_is_queued_at_once` at `sender.rs:1004-1010` and the integration test `agent_messages_are_drafts_the_owner_approves_edits_or_rejects` at `postgres_tests.rs:1078-1110` cover that sending fence. No regression test was found that injects hostile inbound or website text through the ACP prompt renderer.

The audit found a definite reply-to-prompt route, but no automatic website-to-ACP-prompt route in the scoped source. That absence is not a safety proof: browser transcript handling and the live Scout tool configuration were intentionally not run or inventoried. The next security change should cover website text before that route is treated as safe.

### 7. Default outbound behavior

The code default is not autonomous sending by a managed agent. `Access::Outreach` prepares, but does not approve or send, at `whatsapp/mod.rs:26-53`; its outbound message begins as a draft at `sender.rs:307-315`. An owner or admin's own message is considered their approval and begins queued. `docs/whatsapp-outreach.md:288-298` states the same contract. The existing source tests named in the preceding section prove the initial-state distinction, and `only_the_owner_or_an_admin_can_send_now_never_an_agent` at `postgres_tests.rs:2717-2721` covers batch release authority. No test or policy engine was found for the requested domain, instruction-echo, or post-undo holds.

### 8. Thread access and Needs reply

The relay offers a private `thread` query in `api/whatsapp.rs:704-723`, and `whatsapp_list_thread` returns all non-dropped messages subject to an explicit cap of 500 in `store/whatsapp.rs:2031-2052`. The desktop currently asks for 100 at `whatsappOutreachApi.ts:340-351`. The disposable test `a_batch_row_shows_in_the_thread_from_creation_through_the_send_and_the_contact_has_a_name` at `postgres_tests.rs:2246-2289` proves that pending rows appear in a thread, not that a draft always receives complete prior context.

Chats already exposes `Needs reply` only in desk mode at `WhatsAppOutreachContactFilters.tsx:26-41`. It filters an unanswered contact or an existing draft in `WhatsAppOutreachScreen.tsx:303-340`. That is close to the proposed `Awaiting reply` user intent, but it is neither named that way nor coupled to mandatory draft context, and the inbox query excludes opted-out contacts as noted above.

## Real defects noticed, not fixed in this PR

1. **Pause all is non-functional from the desktop.** Both visible controls send `sendingPaused`, while the relay parser and handler ignore it and return a no-change error. This removes an important immediate recovery affordance.
2. **Inbound WhatsApp text can cross an ACP prompt boundary unescaped.** The repository already has an escaping helper for exactly this class of delimiter injection, but generic event and conversation rendering bypass it.
3. **Opt-out handling is too narrow for the stated safety target.** It recognizes exact English whole replies only, provides no undo, and its normal work queue filters out opted-out contacts, including a reply that may contain a complaint.
4. **A reply is contact-scoped, not business-scoped.** A second number or future email for the same business can remain eligible after any first reply.

## Recommended build order

1. **Small: make Pause all real.** Add the typed relay field, durable reason and audit event, then test the real desktop command and atomic worker refusal. This is the fastest owner recovery control.
2. **Medium: close the external-content prompt boundary.** Route every untrusted reply, site extract, email body, and conversation item through one escaped, provenance-labelled renderer. Add hostile-content regression tests before adding more Scout autonomy.
3. **Large: establish canonical outreach identity and suppression.** Implement the shared number, email, domain, contact, and business model; deterministic multilingual opt-out rules; owner undo with re-consent hold; and party-wide reply holds. Make every Discovery and sender path consult it.
4. **Medium: make health pausing policy-driven.** Preserve RED and account failure protection, then add durable failure-window and supported provider-signal rules with conservative resume semantics and clear owner notices.
5. **Medium: complete unknown-send recovery.** Keep the immutable no-resend invariant, add an owner-only check-and-redraft flow, and prove relay persistence plus desktop reload behavior.
6. **Medium: add outbound policy holds and usable reply context.** Evaluate risky links, instruction echo, and post-undo re-contact before queueing. Require fresh or explicitly truncated conversation context for drafts, and expose `Awaiting reply` plus safety escalation in Chats.
